Research & Study

My current work sits around mathematics, physical systems, computation, and uncertainty. Some of it is formal research. Some of it is study. The useful distinction is whether the question is alive enough to keep following.

Does the Model Know Where the Boundary Is?

Epistemic calibration in a dyadic shell model

An independent computational investigation into how under-resolved simulations can distinguish numerical behaviour from evidence of finite-time singularity.

The work is less about claiming a final answer and more about building a disciplined way to ask: when a simulation becomes ambiguous, what exactly do we know, and what are we only mistaking for knowledge?

About

Learning how things work.

I'm a mechanical engineering student at the University of Waterloo.

I tend to become interested in things by trying to understand how they work. Sometimes that means building something. Sometimes it means reading papers for three hours. Sometimes it means writing a simulation and discovering that the simulation has raised more questions than it answered.

My interests currently sit around mathematics, computation, mechanics, numerical methods, and physical systems. They're still evolving, and I like it that way.

I also teach, write, make software, and occasionally disappear into an unnecessarily specific question.

I'm interested in doing good technical work, learning continuously, and finding problems that are difficult enough to be worth thinking about.
