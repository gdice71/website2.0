Does Posterior Uncertainty Widen Across an Open Regularity Interval?

This paper tests whether a Bayesian classifier’s posterior uncertainty widens in a parameter regime where a dyadic shell model’s long-term regularity is unresolved. The model has proven blow-up and regularity zones on either side of that interval, providing a testbed with known boundaries.

The result is exploratory: wider intervals appear in the open zone, but the study does not establish that uncertainty is tracking mathematical undecidability or resolve the Navier–Stokes regularity problem.

Question and approach

The damping exponent α divides the model into a proven finite-time blow-up zone below 1/3 and a proven global-regularity zone at or above 1/2. The interval between them is unresolved. The classifier was trained only on the two proven zones, using features from simulations at several shell resolutions, then evaluated across the full α range.

Bayesian logistic regression used a Gaussian coefficient prior, with posterior sampling by a Metropolis–Hastings sampler implemented for the study. The numerical workflow also compared two time-stepping approaches at one blow-up-regime setting as a solver check.

Methods

  • Resolution sweeps at 15–40 shells
  • Five simulation-derived features
  • Bayesian logistic classifier
  • Five random-seed comparisons
  • Held-out replicate and boundary checks

What the results show

Mean credible-interval width was 0.380 in the open zone, compared with 0.047 in the blow-up zone and 0.029 in the regular zone. The open-zone widening was similar across five random seeds. Held-out replicate predictions achieved 100% accuracy on the labelled zones, including checks near both proven boundaries.

Within the open interval, width peaked around α = 0.37–0.38. The feature-sensitivity peak occurred earlier, around α = 0.25–0.30, which weakens one proposed feature-geometry explanation for the widening.

Interpretation

  • Uncertainty widened where the theoretical label is unresolved.
  • Interpolation between labelled classes could also explain the pattern.
  • A classifier decision boundary or truncated-model behaviour may contribute.
  • No control system without an open problem was tested.
Paper & Limitations

An exploratory first step

The study covers one shell model, one feature family, and one classifier. Since the open zone has no ground-truth labels, the results support the hypothesis only indirectly. They do not establish that posterior uncertainty measures undecidability, and they do not address the open regularity problem itself. A useful next test is a control system with a known transition and no unresolved interval.
