Biomechanics Research

Computational Modeling of Synovial Fluid Mechanics in Osteoarthritis

I am developing a physics-informed inference framework that constrains plausible synovial fluid rheological states using patient-specific knee geometry and biological observations. The pipeline moves from MRI-derived knee geometry through a patient-specific CFD sweep and a Gaussian process surrogate, into a Bayesian inversion that weighs candidate fluid parameters against each patient's own biomarker data.

Rather than claiming to calculate synovial fluid viscosity directly from a scan, the goal is narrower and more defensible: use patient geometry and non-invasive biology to narrow down which rheological states are plausible, and be explicit about where the data leaves real ambiguity.

Question Can patient-specific imaging and biomarkers constrain plausible synovial fluid rheological states through physics-based modeling?
Methods ITK-SNAP segmentation, OpenFOAM CFD (Bird-Carreau rheology), Gaussian process surrogate modeling, Bayesian inversion
Status Segmenting and reconstructing patient-specific knee geometry from OAI MRI data ahead of the CFD sweep

Technical direction

The project splits into two coupled halves. The forward physics half takes patient-specific geometry, segmented from OAI knee MRI in ITK-SNAP, and runs a Latin Hypercube sweep of CFD simulations in OpenFOAM across a range of hypothetical fluid parameters, using a Bird-Carreau (Carreau-Yasuda) description of synovial fluid rather than treating it as a simple Newtonian lubricant. A Gaussian process surrogate is then trained per patient to interpolate between sweep points cheaply.

The inverse half uses that surrogate, together with a cohort-level model linking mechanics to biomarkers, to run a Bayesian inversion: rather than solving for a single "true" viscosity, it produces a posterior distribution over plausible fluid parameters for each patient, conditioned on their own hyaluronic acid and MMP-3 measurements.

Current stage

  • ITK-SNAP segmentation of femur cartilage, tibia cartilage, and joint capsule per patient
  • Mesh cleanup and watertightness checks (MeshLab/Blender, trimesh)
  • Physiological plausibility checks on segmented cavity volumes
  • Pinning down boundary condition and loading assumptions ahead of the CFD sweep

In plain language

A standard MRI can show structural damage in a knee, but it does not directly explain the mechanical state of the joint. This project asks whether computational modeling can use MRI-derived geometry, combined with a patient's own biomarkers, to narrow down how synovial fluid is likely behaving inside an osteoarthritic knee.

The honest output isn't a single number for viscosity, it's a range of plausible fluid behaviors for that patient, weighted by how well each one fits their biology. That range, and how it shifts across a small patient cohort, is what the project treats as the real result.

Framework components

  • MRI-derived, patient-specific knee geometry (ITK-SNAP segmentation)
  • Patient-specific CFD sweep in OpenFOAM (Bird-Carreau rheology, Latin Hypercube sampling)
  • Per-patient Gaussian process surrogate over the sweep
  • Cohort-level mechanics-to-biomarker regression
  • Bayesian inversion producing a posterior over rheological parameters per patient

Why the angle is novel

Most osteoarthritis characterization separates structural imaging, joint biomechanics, and biochemical markers. This project treats the knee as a coupled biological-mechanical system, using a non-identifiability-aware Bayesian framework so that when multiple fluid states explain a patient's biology equally well, that ambiguity shows up honestly as a wide posterior rather than a single overconfident number.

Next steps

  • Finish segmentation and geometry QC across the full patient cohort
  • Run a mesh convergence study and finalize boundary conditions (moving cartilage surfaces, oscillatory loading)
  • Execute the per-patient CFD sweep and train Gaussian process surrogates
  • Run the Bayesian inversion and check inferred rheology against KL grade and MMP-3
