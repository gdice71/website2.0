F1 Floor CAD Design and CFD Analysis

This self-directed study explored how the 2026 FIA Formula 1 floor regulations could affect porpoising and aerodynamic stiffness. Using SimScale CFD and reduced-order Python modeling, the work compared aggressive 2023-style floors against the flatter 2026 concept.

The outcome was a clearer picture of how ride-height sensitivity and pitch behavior change under the newer geometry, with results pointing toward significantly reduced oscillatory behavior.

Focus Ground-effect floor behavior under 2026 regulation changes
Tools CAD, SimScale CFD, Python reduced-order modeling
Result Lower aerodynamic stiffness sensitivity and improved damping

Technical thread

The main question was whether the flatter 2026 floor geometry would reduce the downforce sensitivity that makes porpoising difficult to control. The analysis compared the response of force and pitch characteristics as ride height changed, then used those results to reason about dynamic stability.

It was also an opportunity to develop stronger fluency in post-processing, fitting, and building a cleaner engineering narrative from simulation data.

Current scope

  • Steady RANS CFD at 65 m/s
  • Reduced-order modeling of ride-height effects
  • Ongoing direction toward transient CFD and full-vehicle coupling
